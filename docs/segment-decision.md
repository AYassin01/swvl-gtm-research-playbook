# Why we chose this Swvl segment

**Chosen segment** — Greater Cairo outsourced contact centers that already arrange employee rides for on-site late shifts.

**Primary operator** — Facilities or Transport Manager. **Commercial approval** — Finance or Procurement, according to the account's approval threshold. A named country leader can help route discovery, while their vendor-signing authority remains unconfirmed.

## The decision

| Requirement | Evidence and interpretation | What still needs confirmation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Existing transport demand | Employer job advertisements in the saved account list describe provided rides and overnight work. This is a signal that transport supports staffing. | Whether the screened campaign uses employer-booked routes, an allowance, or individual rides today |
| An accountable buyer workflow | VXI's Cairo Transportation Supervisor description assigns vendor oversight, transport records and cost metrics. | The current named owner, budget, procurement process and contract decision date |
| Identifiable prospects | The original scorecard contains 25 operators with sites, buyer routes or evidence gaps. | Current local operations and independent buying units |
| Credible Swvl delivery | Swvl's 2024 company disclosure describes shift-based contracts for call-center agents. Its current pages describe live tracking and trip reports. | Route coverage, billable units, report fields, fallback responsibility and viable pricing |
| A measurable commercial test | Compare billed runs with trip and boarding records while monitoring pickups on one route. | Whether mismatch, administrative time or service exceptions are material enough to buy |

The VXI role supports the existence of transport-management work. It does not establish fraud or losses at VXI. Swvl's pages are vendor-authored evidence, and their claims about market problems do not establish prevalence at each prospect. The team selected this segment for its observable transport demand, reachable operators and testable buying problem. The saved sample supported discovery. A purchase case requires the buyer's baseline.

## Acceptance gate for future runs

Before calling a new segment accepted for discovery, identify at least 15 distinct relevant companies, at least 15 named current local buyer routes or relevant sponsors with attributable professional sources, and at least three independent employer-side transport signals. Record how each buyer route connects to the actual transport owner. Mark signer authority separately.

Also establish one explicit operational transport workflow or stated failure, one current Swvl delivery source, clear exclusions, and a measurable buyer qualification question. If the count or evidence cannot be met within budget, report an incomplete gate and continue with a narrower hypothesis for team review.

The original saved sample had 14 named routes within its 16 accounts that passed the public screen. This remains below the new 15-buyer release threshold. The baseline therefore stays a historical discovery example with pending review.

## What would make us abandon or reshape the offer

- Buyers report that invoices reconcile easily and the administrative effort is too small to justify switching.
- Relevant campaigns mainly pay allowances or have insufficient recurring rider density.
- Existing contracts, Swvl account ownership, coverage or employee ride policies block the proposed route.
- The accessible people cannot connect the team to an authorized transport owner.

The discovery request is one redacted invoice, the corresponding route log and the buyer's current reconciliation process. Compare scheduled runs with completed and billable runs, then ask how exceptions are resolved and whether a contract decision is open. Any pilot timing, minimum rider count and commercial terms remain proposals until agreed.
